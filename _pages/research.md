## Sicalo Lab – Research Interests

Our research interests center on understanding and managing parasite drug resistance in livestock and other animal hosts. We are particularly interested in how genetic variation, parasite biology, and management practices interact to shape treatment outcomes in real-world settings. Our work emphasizes the integration of field-derived samples, phenotypic drug response data, and genomic information, developed through collaborative and interdisciplinary research.

---

## Primary Areas of Interest

### Parasite drug resistance and treatment response
We are interested in the biological and genetic factors that determine how parasites respond to anthelmintic treatment, and how resistance emerges and spreads under field conditions.

### Linking genotype and phenotype
A major focus of our work is connecting parasite genetic variation to measurable drug response phenotypes, using high-throughput and quantitative approaches developed in collaboration with partner laboratories.

### Field-relevant parasitology
We prioritize research questions driven by veterinary and producer needs, including longitudinal sampling, resistance surveillance, and evaluation of parasite control strategies under commercial management systems.

### Diagnostics and applied decision support
We are interested in developing and refining diagnostic workflows that integrate phenotypic and molecular data to support evidence-based parasite control decisions.

### Training through collaborative science
Our lab provides training opportunities in experimental parasitology, data analysis, and interdisciplinary research, with a strong emphasis on collaboration and translational impact.
